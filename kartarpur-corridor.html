🕌 Gurdwara Darbar Sahib Kartarpur is located in Kartarpur, Shakargarh Tehsil, Narowal District, Punjab, Pakistan — just 4.7 km from the India-Pakistan border. It is the site where Guru Nanak Dev Ji, founder of Sikhism, spent his last 18 years and passed away in 1539. It is considered the second holiest site in Sikhism.

How Indian Pilgrims Can Visit Kartarpur — Step by Step

  • Register online at the official Indian government portal: prakashpurb550.mha.gov.in — register at least 10 days before your visit
  • Pay the USD $20 service fee online during registration. OCI card holders and people of Pakistani origin are exempt from the fee
  • After approval receive your e-Permit via email — no stamping or visa required
  • Travel to Dera Baba Nanak (Gurdaspur district, Punjab India) — nearest airport is Amritsar (Sri Guru Ram Dass Ji Airport)
  • Reach the Integrated Check Post (ICP) at Dera Baba Nanak on the Indian side with your passport and e-Permit
  • Clear Indian immigration and security checks at the ICP
  • Walk or take the bus shuttle across the 4.2 km Kartarpur Corridor into Pakistan
  • Clear Pakistan immigration at the Pakistan terminal — passport stamped
  • Take the shuttle bus from Pakistan terminal to Gurdwara Darbar Sahib Kartarpur (free shuttle)
  • Visit the Gurdwara, participate in Langar, and explore the complex. Return before designated cut-off time
⚠️ Important: Kartarpur Corridor is a day visit only. Indian pilgrims must return to India the same day. Overnight stay in Pakistan is not permitted via this corridor. Always carry your original passport — no photocopies accepted.

From Lahore by car: Take M-11 Lahore-Sialkot Motorway, exit at Narowal interchange, continue on Shakargarh Road towards Kartarpur. The Gurdwara complex is well signposted. Parking is available near the complex.

By public transport from Lahore: Take a wagon or coach to Shakargarh from Lahore (Thokar Niaz Baig). From Shakargarh take a local wagon or rickshaw to Kartarpur (PKR 100–150).

What to See at Kartarpur Complex

🕌
Gurdwara Darbar Sahib
The main shrine — a magnificent white marble Gurdwara built by Maharaja Ranjit Singh. The holiest spot where Guru Nanak is believed to have passed away in 1539.
🍛
Langar (Free Community Kitchen)
Free vegetarian food served to all visitors regardless of religion 24 hours a day. One of the largest Langars in the world — a Sikh tradition of equality.
🏛️
Sarovar (Holy Pool)
A large sacred pool inside the complex where Sikh pilgrims perform ritual bathing. The pool is beautifully maintained within the Gurdwara complex.
🌾
Guru Nanak's Farm (Kikar Tree)
The historic farmland where Guru Nanak spent his final years farming and teaching. A sacred Kikar tree from Guru Nanak's era still stands on the grounds.
🏗️
Passenger Terminal
Modern state-of-the-art passenger terminal with immigration facilities, waiting lounges, shops, and restaurants for pilgrims crossing from India.
🎭
Museum & Exhibition
Fakir Chand Museum display inside the complex showcasing the history of Guru Nanak, Sikhism, and the Kartarpur Corridor project. Free to visit.

History of Kartarpur & the Corridor

1504 AD
Guru Nanak founds Kartarpur
Guru Nanak Dev Ji, founder of Sikhism, established the town of Kartarpur on the right bank of River Ravi and set up the first Sikh commune. People of all faiths lived and farmed together.
1539 AD
Guru Nanak passes away at Kartarpur
Guru Nanak spent his last 18 years at Kartarpur farming, teaching and building community. He passed away here in 1539. The original Gurdwara was built at this site.
19th Century
Maharaja Ranjit Singh rebuilds the Gurdwara
The Sikh ruler Maharaja Ranjit Singh reconstructed the Gurdwara Darbar Sahib Kartarpur after the original was washed away by River Ravi floods.
1947
Partition — Kartarpur goes to Pakistan
After the partition of India and Pakistan, Shakargarh Tehsil (including Kartarpur) was allocated to Pakistan. Indian Sikhs were separated from their holiest shrine by the border.
November 9, 2019
Kartarpur Corridor inaugurated
Prime Minister Imran Khan inaugurated the Kartarpur Corridor — a historic visa-free passage allowing Indian Sikhs to visit the Gurdwara for the first time in 72 years. Guru Nanak's 550th birth anniversary was celebrated.
2020–2021
COVID closure and reopening
The corridor was temporarily closed due to COVID-19 in March 2020 and gradually reopened in 2021 with health protocols.
2026
Corridor fully operational
Kartarpur Corridor is fully operational welcoming thousands of Sikh pilgrims from India, UK, Canada, USA and worldwide every month.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How to visit Kartarpur Corridor from India in 2026?▼
Register online at prakashpurb550.mha.gov.in at least 10 days before your visit. Pay the $20 USD fee online. Get your e-Permit via email. Travel to Dera Baba Nanak ICP in Gurdaspur, Punjab India with your passport and e-Permit. Cross the 4.2 km corridor into Pakistan. The Gurdwara shuttle is free from the Pakistan terminal.
What is the fee for Kartarpur Corridor 2026?▼
USD $20 per person per visit for Indian pilgrims. OCI card holders are exempt. Pakistani citizens enter free with CNIC. The $20 fee is paid online during registration on the Indian government portal. The fee goes to the Pakistan government as service charges.
Can Pakistani Muslims visit Kartarpur Gurdwara?▼
Yes, absolutely. Gurdwara Darbar Sahib Kartarpur welcomes all visitors regardless of religion — Muslims, Christians, Hindus, Sikhs. Pakistani citizens enter free with their CNIC. No prior registration is needed. Head covering is required for all visitors.
How long does a Kartarpur visit take?▼
Most visitors spend 2-4 hours at the complex including the Gurdwara darshan, Langar (free food), and exploring the grounds. Indian pilgrims must return the same day. Plan to arrive by late morning to have enough time before the cut-off for returning to India.
What is the nearest airport to Kartarpur Pakistan?▼
For visitors coming to Kartarpur via Pakistan, the nearest airport is Allama Iqbal International Airport, Lahore (120 km, 2 hours). Sialkot Airport is also close (about 70 km). For Indian pilgrims, Sri Guru Ram Dass Ji International Airport in Amritsar is the nearest airport on the Indian side.
Is Langar (free food) available at Kartarpur?▼
Yes. Langar — the Sikh tradition of free community kitchen — is served at Gurdwara Darbar Sahib Kartarpur to all visitors 24 hours a day, regardless of religion. The food is vegetarian. It is one of the largest Langars in the world and is completely free for everyone.
How far is Kartarpur from Narowal city?▼
Kartarpur is approximately 35 km from Narowal city, about 40 minutes by car via Shakargarh Road. From Shakargarh city it is about 15 km and 20 minutes. Local wagons and rickshaws are available from Shakargarh to Kartarpur.
